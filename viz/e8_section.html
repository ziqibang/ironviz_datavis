The reasoning model handled 10 of every 100 requests but cost 65 of every 100 dollars. The small/fast model handled 42 requests for about 1 dollar. A reasoning request cost about $0.25 more and used 4.8 Wh more (range 3.5–6.2) than a frontier request.
The habit that outlasts the modelUse a reasoning model for complex, multi-step problems. For everything else, OpenAI advises: Speed and cost → GPT models are faster and tend to cost less.

Three 10 by 10 grids comparing three OpenAI model types in October 2026. Requests: reasoning 10, frontier 48, small/fast 42. Dollars: reasoning 65, frontier 34, small/fast 1. Estimated watt-hours: reasoning 32, frontier 42, small/fast 26.

Data: CMU IronViz synthetic telemetry, Oct 1–30, 2026; 103,211 successful requests from the three OpenAI teams (the Gemini teams have no reasoning model in the data). Squares are rounded so each grid adds to 100. Cost: public list prices (the o1-preview price is from a third-party archive and unverified). Energy: estimated from Jegham et al. (2025), arXiv:2505.09598, and Elsworth et al. (2025), arXiv:2508.15734; the o1 benchmark stands in for o1-preview. Guidance: OpenAI, Reasoning best practices, developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning-best-practices, accessed Nov 14, 2026. All three models have since been retired or scheduled for retirement; the habit applies to whichever models are current.

Strengths

  • All three model types are named, colored and explained, so the comparison is explicit.
  • The same 100-square format shows requests, cost and energy side by side.
  • Shows the small/fast model doing 42% of the work for about 1% of the cost.

Weaknesses

  • More to read than the orange-only version.
  • OpenAI only (Gemini has no reasoning model in the data).
